Partner next steps before product launch

What to do before there's anything to sell

Four tracks of useful work. All executable right now. Click tasks to track progress.

Track 1 — Build the prospect list
When we're ready to sell, this list is everything. Building it now means day-one momentum.
Map your existing network for salon/spa owners
Who do you already know? Warm intros are 10x easier than cold outreach. Start there.
Research 20–30 target businesses in your area
Google Maps, Yelp, Instagram. Find owner-operated salons with 2–10 staff. Note their tools (Vagaro, Square, etc.) where visible.
Find the owner's name and contact for each
Instagram bio, Google business listing, LinkedIn. The goal is a name, not just a business — you're selling to a person.
Build it in a simple spreadsheet — name, business, contact, notes
Don't overthink the format. Four columns is enough. The habit of capturing it matters more than the tool.
Track 2 — Do discovery conversations
Talking to owners before you pitch them is the single most useful thing you can do. You learn what they actually care about — and they start to trust you before you ever ask for money.
Have 5 casual conversations with salon/spa owners
Not a pitch. Genuine curiosity. "How do you keep track of your business performance?" "What's your biggest headache right now?"
Ask about their current tools and what frustrates them
What do they use? Do they actually look at the analytics? What question do they wish they could answer easily?
Write up what you hear after each conversation
Even just a paragraph. The patterns that emerge across 5 conversations will shape how we pitch and what language we use.
Share notes back with the founder
This is product research as much as it is sales groundwork. The feedback loop matters.
Track 3 — Develop the sales pitch
The pitch needs to exist before you can use it. Building it now, with the founder's input, means it's ready the moment there's something to show.
Write a one-paragraph version of the pitch in your own words
How would you explain Pulse to a salon owner over coffee? Write it like you'd say it — not like a brochure. Share it with the founder to refine.
Anticipate the top 5 objections and draft responses
"I already have Square analytics." "I don't have time to learn another tool." "What does this actually cost?" Better to work these out now than on the call.
Draft a short cold outreach message
DM, email, or text — whatever fits the audience. Keep it to 3–4 sentences. The goal is a conversation, not a sale.
Role-play the pitch with the founder at least once
Have them play a skeptical salon owner. The awkward moments in practice are the ones that would have killed real calls.
Track 4 — Know the landscape
Prospects will mention competitors. You need to know what's out there, why Pulse is different, and how to say that without trashing anyone.
Spend 30 minutes with Square, Vagaro, and 7shifts
Look at their analytics sections specifically. What do they show? Where do they fall short? This makes you credible when owners bring them up.
Look up Thryv and Broadly briefly
These are the "replace everything" platforms. Know why Pulse is positioned differently — we connect, we don't replace.
Write one sentence for each competitor: "They do X, we do Y"
Simple, honest, not aggressive. This becomes your handle whenever a prospect asks "how is this different from what I already have?"
The goal of all of this: by the time there's something to demo, you walk in with a warm list, a tested pitch, real insight into what owners care about, and a clear answer for every objection. That's not prep work — that's a head start most sales people don't have.
